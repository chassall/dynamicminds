Datasets

We believe that openly sharing EEG data through repositories such as OpenNeuro is important for both scientific and practical reasons.

  1. Reproducibility. Many EEG findings are based on complex preprocessing pipelines and statistical analyses. When raw data are publicly available, other researchers can verify whether the reported results hold up when the same or different analysis methods are applied. This helps address concerns about reproducibility in psychology and neuroscience.
  2. Discovery. EEG datasets are expensive and time-consuming to collect. Sharing allows researchers to ask new questions that were not part of the original study. This includes the development of new methods.
  3. Transparency. Data sharing allows readers, reviewers, and other scientists to see exactly what evidence supports a published claim.
  4. Training. Students can learn EEG analysis using real, curated datasets rather than artificial examples. For example, in an ongoing collaboration with Dr. Dana Cobzas, computer science students at MacEwan can access and analyze our data using recent machine-learning techniques.
